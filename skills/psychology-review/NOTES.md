## What this can tell a director

- The search for studies varying a character's attractiveness, assertiveness, kindness or aggression against the force of a
  surprise, misdirection or learning moment was small and found none [P37]. None of [P37]'s guesses was measured for fiction.

## The review form

- *Short study* includes a 13-sentence story.
- The producer staffs tests; qa or a playtest owner runs them and reports counts. qa checks acceptance, the director and designer
  decide, the writer changes lines.

## Where learning and story pull apart

- Aha and truth: people rated trivia claims truer when solving an anagram completed the claim, false ones as well as true, most on
  trials where they reported an aha (d = .629 across both, 69 people). Solving and aha weren't assigned at random, and the
  randomised comparison found no overall effect [P35].
- Committed theory: explaining a theory about a character made it survive discrediting [P33].
- Prediction before surprise: the surprise was pupil-measured [P36].
- Reveal: in 13-sentence stories, readers not asked to judge kept a character's trait model when it was only refuted, and revised
  with a causal explanation (reading times). Readers told to judge outcomes also revised after a bare refutation [P31].

## Reading characters

- Attractiveness: in a meta-analysis of rated traits the halo was largest on social competence and near zero on integrity [P23].
  On face photos attractiveness judgments correlated .79 with trustworthiness judgments [P24]. In one lab game attractive trustees
  were viewed as more trustworthy and trusted more [P23]. The one penalty seen fell on attractive players who under-delivered in a
  lab game, not on a broken bond.
- Assertiveness: too little or too much both lowered leader ratings; force and intimidation gained influence and gaze and weren't
  liked; compared side by side, the warmer target was assumed less competent [P25].
- Betrayal analogies: bonds go with both forgiveness and lasting grudges [P26, P27].

## Motivation

- Within a person, past performance predicted later self-efficacy (ρ = .40) more than self-efficacy predicted performance (.23,
  or .06 with the trend controlled) [P6].
- Fluency: a prepared, fluent lecturer raised predicted recall and ratings against a halting one, not recall 10 minutes later
  (65-second video, 21 and 35 undergraduates a condition). Being told a lecturer was warm raised ratings; a positive instructor
  raised a delayed test but not an immediate one [P17].
- Wise feedback raised revision among African American 7th graders, most among those mistrusting school (44 students in one
  trial). Among White students the authors call any effect "small and less robust". Real teachers on real essays, given only where
  credible. Watching rudeness wasn't read [P19].

## Traits and groups

- Dominance cost women against men on likeability (d = -0.19, k = 63): explicit d = -.28, implicit d = 0.03, competence d = 0.02.
  Stimuli not in the abstract, framed around leaders [P38]. One leader-vignette study found it varies by race [P39].
- Randomising protagonist gender among 3,000 US adults moved story choice +0.008 for men, +0.062 for women [P40].

## Limits on tactics

- Mild dark patterns more than doubled sign-up without backlash [P42].

## Tests

- With 6 people a version, a read held by 30% shows up at least once 88% of the time, by 10% only 47%. 5 of 6 against 1 of 6:
  Fisher p = .08.
