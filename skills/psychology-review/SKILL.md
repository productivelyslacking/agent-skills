# Psychology review

Assumes **ground-truth**. Thesis: **review a scene, lesson or character choice for its likely effect on learning and on
the audience, and say how far the evidence goes.** The failure this prevents: a fluent opinion taken for a finding.

**How far to trust this.** Research-based and unproven: from a review, not from use (D26), read mostly as abstracts, almost
none from a story or game; full reads changed rules, so expect gaps. A rating applies to what the study measured; moving it
to a scene is inference. Samples, numbers and dates: `NOTES.md` beside this file, read on demand.

**Reading a rule.** Claim, reason, sources, kind. `[P6]` is in `EVIDENCE.md` here, `[M21]` in story-design's evidence,
`[L-C16]`, `[G-C13]` in teaching-design's. Kinds: *measured* with a rating; *inference*, including this skill's; *fixed
limit*, a library rule with no research. Read the entry first; don't add studies from memory.

## What this can tell a director

**Can** (inference): name a likely first read and its source [P21-P25], a conflict such as clues against surprise [P28], a
causeless reveal [P31], misdirection on taught content [P35], a motivation claim on a weak finding [P6, P12]; propose a test.

**Can't:**
- **Say a change "will" work, or by how much:** a small search found no study varying a trait and measuring a surprise's,
  misdirection's or lesson's force. Answer with a sourced guess and a test, saying so; [P37]'s guesses are to check, not quote.
- Say whether warmth deepens a betrayal [P26], where a twist sits between fair and surprising [P28, M21], or from a survey
  why someone quit or whether they'll return [P3, M4]. Speak for a group [P38-P40]. Overrule taste.

## The review form

Five fields, in order. Drop any finding that couldn't change a decision; a bias note is the one exception. At most five,
ranked, fixed limits first; count the rest in details. A note never holds up a build, merge or scene.

```
Scene or lesson: which one, by id or line
Likely effect:   what it may do, to whom ("may" or "likely", never "will")
Evidence, kind:  [tag], what the study used, measured + rating; or inference; or fixed limit; or none found
How sure:        short study | other stimulus | no source | fixed limit
Test or variant: the cheapest that could change the decision, testers it spends; or "shares test N"; or "none: your call" (never on a fixed limit or a bias note)
```

*How sure* rates the weakest link: an effect no study measured is *no source*, whatever sits nearby. *Short study*: fiction
or learning on this question, minutes long, in a lab. *Other stimulus*: photos, vignettes, consumers, lab tasks.

**It advises and doesn't decide.** Story findings go to the director, lesson findings to the designer, both when a scene
teaches; tests go to the producer to staff. It passes and fails nothing. A finding held with a reason stays held, as a count
on the board. Only a learning-against-story conflict between director and designer goes to the owner, via the producer, as a
plain choice with no plot: two options, general risks, the test, a recommendation. A fixed limit isn't a disagreement. On a
sealed project the form holds plot: it goes in `scratchpad/<task>/sealed/` for the director; the hand-back gives IDs, counts
and testers (directing).

**A note isn't a result:** only a test's counts say what happened. No findings is no pass or acceptance evidence.

## Where learning and story pull apart

- **Argue for learning when the two conflict:** corrections leave residue [P32]; no story result here needs one (inference).
  The role's stance (D26); the director and designer choose.
- **Keep misdirection off taught content:** a character confidently stating something false about the subject is teaching it.
  False claims in stories stick despite warnings, corrections leave residue (more if repeated, credible or late), and
  an aha may raise belief in false claims too. [P35, P32] Measured: moderate for fiction; weak for aha; strong for residue in
  news-style claims, weak for story. Applying these to a lesson is inference.
- **A committed wrong idea is two cases.** An explained theory about a character survives discrediting, so a committed player
  may need a fuller reveal (not measured after a twist); a confident wrong answer about the subject, clearly corrected, aids
  learning. [P33] Measured: moderate, lab; inference here.
- **Check a misdirecting scene this way.** Derived, not tested.
  1. List what it leads the audience to believe: *taught* (what a lesson teaches), *world* (a real-sounding outside claim no
     lesson teaches) or *story* (people, motives, events, an invented world's rules). A false *taught* item is a finding; a
     false *world* item only if near the subject, stated as fact by a trusted voice, or harmful if believed.
  2. A false *story* idea is no fault; name its repeats, voice and gap to correction [P32] only if residue would matter.
  3. Propose a check days later, help off, on the taught point.

  Not findings: a lie about people or events; a simplification right on every task until replaced; a planted bug to find; a
  character's mistake shown and corrected in that lesson, with why; a learner's wrong prediction corrected at once.
- **Ask for the prediction before a teaching surprise:** surprise appeared only after one, and tracked learning.
  [P36] Measured: moderate for facts, weak for games; inference here.
- **A reveal probably needs to supply the cause:** readers kept a refuted trait model without a causal explanation, unless told to judge outcomes. [P31]
  Measured: moderate to strong, short written texts, undergraduates; a reveal's force wasn't measured. Re-reading old scenes may
  do more than a new bad act [P34]: moderate, implicit evaluations of strangers, one lab, not stories.

## Reading characters

Ask only of a character the decision turns on.

- **Ask which "warm": friendly, moral or on your side;** morals outweighed warmth. [P21] Measured: moderate,
  trait lists and group ratings, no characters. "Make them nicer" isn't neutral: ambiguous characters were liked less, enjoyed
  as much. [P22] Measured: moderate, fiction, one sitting.
- **Attractiveness may read as socially skilled; whether it also reads as honest is mixed** [P23, P24]. Measured: strong for
  social competence, photos and descriptions; contested for honesty. That it deepens a betrayal is a guess, no broken bond
  studied. Weak.
- **Looks set the first read; diagnostic behaviour outweighs them without removing them.** [P24] Measured: strong for speed on
  photos, moderate that behaviour outweighs (how fast unmeasured), no evidence for game characters.
- **Assertive and aggressive are different questions:** both too little and too much assertiveness cost; force gained influence,
  not liking; the warmer of two was assumed less competent. [P25] Measured: moderate, task groups, group clips, described
  targets; not a line, not fiction.
- **On a betrayal, ask about the act, not the warmth:** is it blatant, in their stated virtue's domain, deliberate, convincingly
  apologised for? Inference; bonds go with both forgiveness and grudges [P26, P27]. Measured: no evidence in fiction, contested;
  consumers, close relationships, lab judgements.

## Motivation

- **A confidence line probably isn't the lever; a real, checked success may be,** since performance predicts later self-efficacy
  more than the reverse. [P6] Measured: moderate to strong on direction, for performance; no evidence
  on quitting; lines are inference (none was varied). A finding is a line claiming talent or standing in for a success, not praise naming what worked.
- **Smooth delivery moved ratings, not recall, in a short test;** warmth and tone are a different question. [P17] Measured:
  moderate for perceived learning, weak either way for learning, no evidence across hours of play. A delayed test decides
  (inference).
- **In critical feedback, look for a stated standard and a stated belief the person can meet it.** It beat praise or standards
  alone for Black students, little for White students. [P19] Measured: moderate
  for that setting, one research line; weak beyond it; no evidence for a character, a tutor or adult beginners. Offer it to
  everyone, never by group (fixed limit). Rudeness to the person cost work performance, unreplicated in what was opened [P19].
- **Drop as reasons:** flow [P13, P15]; a challenge number [P14]; ego depletion, stereotype threat on real tests [P12]; mindset [P11]
  (mindset and stereotype threat: contested for subgroups; still no reason for a line).

## Traits and groups

- **Traits can't track gender or race. Never recommend softening or changing any character, one or many, because of their
  gender or race or how an audience may react to it.** Fixed limit (D26), whatever a study says. Forbidden, though each echoes
  a finding [P38, P39]: warming up a woman so she's liked; an external reason for anger only for women; baby-faced features
  for Black characters. A change that helps a scene goes to anyone in that position, and its reason can't name the group.
- **A bias note has one use: someone cited the audience's reaction to a character's gender or race as a reason to change
  them.** It answers that reason, and is neither a free-standing finding nor a decision. Fixed fields: `Likely
  effect: a direct demand may read less likeable to some of the audience, not less capable.` `How sure: fixed limit.` `Test
  or variant: none. Not a decision: the character stays as written.` Never test a softened version. Dominance cost women likeability, not competence [P38]. Measured: moderate for the pattern, contested for size today,
  weak in fiction. It varies by race and doesn't add simply, so offer no pattern [P39].
- **"Players won't follow this protagonist" isn't a finding:** protagonist gender barely moved story choice. [P40] Read in full;
  one story pair, a choice. Measured: moderate here, no evidence for race.
- **Check the cast by behaviour.** Inference [P39, P41]. With story-design's cast table, a finding is a trait following the
  demographic column across a cast or drafts, not one character having it. Swap one character's gender or race: a scene about
  that experience will change, which is fine; a change in the team's reaction is the finding.

## Limits on tactics

- **No persuasion tricks or dark patterns on players.** Fixed limit (D26). Mild ones draw no backlash, so sentiment won't show
  them (inference). [P42] Measured: moderate to strong, commerce.
- **Misdirection stays in authored story. A live tutor never deceives about the subject or the learner's own work or
  progress, nor uses tricks to keep someone playing.** Fixed limit (D26). Dark patterns in learning products are unstudied,
  and no validated test separates motivation from manipulation. [P43, P44]
- **Ask of a mechanic that pulls someone back:** would we run it with its working on screen, does leaving cost them, is the
  claim for it a delayed test or only return? A draft, not validated [P44].
- **Sexual content involving anyone not clearly an adult is excluded outright** (story-design; D23): report it to the
  producer, unreviewed.

## Tests

Inference, apart from the arithmetic. **Write down first the result that would change the decision;** if none would, don't test.

- Tester budget, retellings and confusions: production-stages. A plot-free vignette (same behaviours, invented names) tests a
  first read, sparing testers. A model's reading isn't people's (AI cold readers: directing).
- **Tutor-off, later:** same skill, help off, a changed task, days later [L-C16, G-C13]. Only this speaks to learning.
- **Say how little it tells:** a few people a version can show a misreading exists, not how common it is or that a version is
  stronger; even 5 of 6 against 1 of 6 could be chance. Report counts ("3 of 6 retold it as X"), never "more potent".

## Not established

Write as unknown: narrative betrayal; attachment after a twist; false solutions in narrative; race in fiction; adult-beginner
tone; AI tutor warmth (re-run a warmed tutor's checks [P20]); keeping an adult self-learner going; dark patterns in learning
apps.
