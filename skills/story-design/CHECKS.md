# Story checks

For **story-design**, read when reviewing a draft. Every item is a derived rule, not measured: nobody tested these
checks. Tags are entries in `EVIDENCE-MEASURED.md` and `EVIDENCE-CRAFT.md`, whose spoiler warning applies. Keep the
results beside the draft so a reviewer can check them.

## Audit a draft

1. **Repeats.** Search for Elias, Mara, Elara, keeper, baker, mayor, clockmaker, fisherman, librarian, conductor,
   lighthouse [C75] and Elena Vasquez, Marcus Chen, Amara Okafor, Aris Thorne, Lena Petrova, Elara Voss [C76]; replace
   or justify each hit. The first list comes from a bare "write a story" prompt, so a tight brief may differ. Lists go
   stale, so also scan a batch from the current model.
2. **Question ledger.** Each question posed has the scene that answers it, or is marked as deliberately never answered.
   [M2, C12, M24]
3. **Reveal table.** Each reveal lists the earlier lines indicating it and the world rule it uses; any concealing
   narrator's lines touching it are literally true. [C15, C17, C50]
4. **Cast table.** Over a whole story, per significant character, named or not: gender and race as written
   ("unspecified" is an answer), own goal, power held, guilty or not, harmed or dead and for whose story. Write one line
   on any column where one group holds all the guilt, harm or lack of goals: change it or say why. [C58a, C58b, C58e]
5. **Content.** Each violent, sexual or profane beat names its job and fits the written limit and the fixed exclusion.
   [C72, C62a]
6. **Stated intentions.** Mark lines where a character states their intention or motive outright, and keep only those
   that do a job a subtler line can't. One user remark on one tool. [C80]
7. **Payoffs.** Over a whole story, not a chapter, list each setup and the scene that pays it off; a late payoff with no
   earlier setup, or a thread dropped at the end, needs a reason. Inference from [C74], whose measure was a uniqueness
   score, not events.
8. **Relations.** List who is hostile or indifferent to whom; an all-warm list needs a reason. [C78]

## Check a first chapter

Derived rules, not measured. Passing doesn't show anyone will continue, and a miss is a question for the writer, not a
fault: a slow opening can be right. Record the answers.

1. **Retelling.** Someone new to it retells the chapter, says what they most want answered, and offers one possible
   answer. That question is the one the chapter means to raise. Use at least three people; a model reading it doesn't
   count, and if none are available, record the check as not run. [C48, M1]
2. **Paid and open.** At least one question closes inside the chapter, and at least one is open at the last line. [M2,
   M8]
3. **Promises.** Each thing the chapter implies about the story, including tone and content, names the later chapter
   that delivers it or deliberately subverts it. [C40, C49, M34]
4. **Return.** Count who starts the next chapter, straight away and after a gap, against earlier builds. A survey
   doesn't replace it. Before release, write down how this will be counted; a first build has no comparison, so it
   reports a number, not a verdict. [M15] Nothing measured this for a story; M9's outcome was minutes per scheduled
   visit.
