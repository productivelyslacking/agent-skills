## How far to trust this

Written on 2026-11-06 from a review, not from use (D23).

## What isn't known

- **Return [M9, M12]:** 44 children aged 8 to 12, in visits the study scheduled and paid for, played longer per visit with
  a serial. Adults visited a gym more when page-turner audiobooks were kept there, and it faded.
- **Absorption [M4, M5]:** in a game, flow and narrative engagement correlated nearly perfectly.
- **Zeigarnik [M11]:** recall ratio 0.99; people resume lab tasks about 67% of the time.
- **Emotional direction [M40]:** film ratings and article reading, with experiments on evaluations; stronger for
  thrillers than romance.

## Openings and chapter endings

- **Timing of the question [C1, C4, C11]:** the one figure is an inciting incident near 10% of runtime in ten top films,
  from one coder with no inter-rater check. Field's first act is a film template; the teaser before the credits comes
  from TV study guides.
- **Promises [C13, C40, C49]:** one game's trouble is put down to midgame feints that promised another story; another set
  out to convince players it was a different game, and had critics for it.
- **What a first chapter drew [M34]:** the violent descriptions were TV blurbs.
- **Chapter endings [M9]:** among the children, serial parts sustained play where self-contained ones declined.
- **Paying each chapter [M2]:** resolution is half of what was measured.
- **Act-outs [C11, C24]:** a peak may matter more for continuing at once than across a gap. Testers playing scenarios
  apart forgot or lacked earlier context.
