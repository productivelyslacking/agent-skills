## Agency and fixed plots

- **Fixed plots [C31, C40]:** Ingold, asked how to make choices matter, quipped "You don't", then called choices a tool
  for buy-in and responsibility. Short places one fixed-ending game's trouble in midgame feints, not its ending.
- **Bad options [M17]:** almost nobody took them, so that cell was dropped.
- **Patterns [C30, C34]:** branch-and-bottleneck without state as likely a gauntlet is Ashwell's reading.
- **How much to branch [C27, C31, CD6]:** Ingold says far less than it looks; Kurvitz goes deep, at about a million
  words.
- **Showing state changes [C32, CD5]:** Ingold hides them; others display them.

## Play and plot as one action

- **Contradiction [C25]:** Takumi's finding, from a fan translation.
- **Mechanic against story [C37, C38, C39, CD7]:** Hocking: it can break a game. Druckmann: doesn't buy it, for a
  stylised adventure. Williams: make it the point, and let characters rationalise it.
- **Forced actions [C37, C47, C49]:** one player felt mocked.
- **First action [C39, C45]:** one game personalises a fixed lead with small prologue choices that pay off in view.

## Must-reach and must-reason

- **Which step [C21, CD2]:** Laws makes finding automatic; Alexander says this mechanises deduction too.
- **Stall events [C20, C41]:** Alexander calls the last-resort close "usually less than satisfactory".
- **Red herrings [C20, CD3]:** Alexander, on tabletop play where players must act on a conclusion: they latch on and
  resist re-assessing, so "only ... with extreme caution".
- **Technicalities [C24, C29]:** Klavins counts thirty minutes of being stuck that way as bad.
- **Batches [C23]:** one game confirms fates in threes.
- **Playtest methods [C24, C26, C48, CD15]:** Klavins interviews, Barlow crunches numbers then tests, Valve asks for a
  retelling.

## First-person and unseen protagonists

- **Silence [C43]:** Laidlaw doesn't recommend it for its difficulties.
- **Large casts [C44]:** a critic's account of a silent lead.
- **Tourist [C46]:** as in one game: they witness others' stories and aren't the centre.
- **Change in delivery [C47]:** one game changes barks and animations at scripted points.
- **Customisation [M29]:** a one-minute daily breathing exercise.

## Player-directed romance

- **Not into the player [C63, CD10]:** Gaider says you can't do it both ways, prefers characters with their own
  motives, and calls players who want the opposite justified; Larian shipped the other way to praise.
- **Coin-slot romance [C64, C65]:** critics' term for romance as a reward for enough chats.
- **Non-romance relationships [C65]:** a Larian writer wanted equal scenes, and it was cut for time.

## Voice and still images

- **Timing [C54, CW10]:** 150 words a minute is a vendor rule of thumb.
- **Generated voice test (one project):** a round compared voices with each other, and no pair could be confused, while
  the owner heard the voices stop sounding like one person from line to line. That round's speaker similarity across a
  voice's own lines ran from 0.41 to 0.76, with no same-take control to read it against. A later tool scored lower on it
  and the owner called its voices much better.
