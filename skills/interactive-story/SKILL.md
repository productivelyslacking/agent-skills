# Interactive story

Assumes **story-design** (general rules, audit, meaning of each kind). Thesis: **what the player does must be a fact in
the plot, written so the plot still moves when they do it badly**, not a story and a game side by side, or a story that
stops dead on one missed clue.

**How far to trust this.** Research-based and unproven: from a review, not use (D23); its first real test is a story
game's first playtest. Mostly practitioner craft from famous, finished works, with no failed attempts in the sample.
Tags point to `../story-design/EVIDENCE-MEASURED.md` (`[M17]`) and `EVIDENCE-CRAFT.md` (`[C19]`, `[CD1]`, `[CW7]`),
whose spoiler warning applies. Nothing here shows any of this keeps a player playing. `NOTES.md` here holds who said
what, the numbers and the dates.

## Agency and fixed plots

- **A fixed plot is defensible; a broken promise isn't.** Choices can be a tool for buy-in and responsibility rather
  than branching, and one fixed-ending game's trouble is put on midgame feints, not its ending. No practitioner
  statement on keeping investment in a fixed ending was found. [C31, C40, CW7] Practitioner and critic. The check is
  inference.
- **Don't treat illusory choice as free.** Feedback without branching kept felt agency on most items, yet players
  noticed the story wouldn't have differed, and feedback wasn't shown to be the cause. [M17] Measured, weak that real
  branching is unnecessary.
- **Spend choice writing on visibly different situations**, which were rated more agentic. [M17] Measured, one study (n
  = 88), no separate rating; applying it where branching can't be afforded is inference.
- **Don't expect interactivity to raise engagement by itself**: experiments adding it often found no difference. [M17]
  Measured, contested, no evidence on continuation.
- **Don't assume players take a bad option, nor that it's wasted**: in the one experiment almost nobody did, and the
  more moral identified less with the lead. [M17] Measured, one study, sample not read; budgeting on it is inference.
- **Name each chapter's pattern**: a fixed plot with reactive state is a gauntlet plus state, and branch-and-bottleneck
  without state likely a gauntlet; storylets suit reactive side content around a spine, not a one-solution mystery.
  [C30, C34] Practitioner and critic; inference.
- **How much to branch: budget likely decides**; practitioners range from far less than it looks to very deep. [C27,
  C31, CD6] Practitioner, contested; inference.
- **Keep plot state apart from skill and resource variables**, or useful distinctions are erased. [C35] Practitioner,
  single.
- **Decide once per project whether state changes are shown, and test it**: mostly taste, with no condition for
  choosing. [C32, CD5] Practitioner, contested; testing it is inference.
- **If a system picks lines from what the player does, expect mistimed lines and trial-and-error weights.** [C36]
  Practitioner, single.
- **Log skips and completion per beat**: no published skip rate was found, so your log is data the literature lacks.
  [M16] Measured, no evidence; inference.

## Play and plot as one action

- **For each task or puzzle result, not each input or dialogue tone choice, write what it establishes in the plot and,
  in a mystery, who it implicates**, or story and task sit side by side. Well-regarded games do this; no study shows it
  improves story. [C39, C23, C25, C26] Demonstrated, famous cases only; inference. For learning products see
  **teaching-design**.
- **In a mystery, build scenes around a stated contradiction that an action exposes**: one designer found contradiction
  produces turns automatically. [C25] Practitioner, fan translation.
- **Decide by tone how much mechanic and story may disagree.** Practitioners range from "it can break a game" to "make
  it the point"; a serious mystery likely tolerates less. [C37, C38, C39, CD7] Practitioner, contested; tone as the
  condition is inference.
- **Don't blame the player for an action you forced unless that is the design**; the deliberate version still has
  critics. [C37, C47, C49] Practitioner and critic; inference.
- **In a first chapter, make the first action's result prove something, and pay off a small early choice visibly**, as
  one game personalises a fixed lead. Nothing measures whether this holds players. [C39, C45] Demonstrated, student
  account; inference.
- **Name what the difficulty is for, rather than defaulting to hard**: nearly every source treats being stuck on a
  technicality as the failure. [C29] Inference, from famous works.
- **If budget allows, author reactions to common wrong answers**, so a failed attempt is story, not only a retry. [C27]
  Demonstrated at a budget few have; inference.

## Must-reach and must-reason

Clue advice covers two cases, so it looks contradictory [CD1]; inference, nobody tested the split. Label every
conclusion first.

| | Must-reach: the plot needs it | Must-reason: it is the puzzle |
|---|---|---|
| Clues | List at least three routes, so two are backups: plans don't survive players [C19, C22] | Remove any clue that skips the steps [C24] |
| A failed attempt | Never depends on one successful attempt [C19] | May block, within the pacing rule below [C24] |
| Evidence | Practitioner, tabletop; a GM improvises and a script can't | Practitioner, single, playtest interviews |

- **Decide which step the player does: finding, connecting or concluding**; each is its own failure point. Whether
  automatic finding mechanises deduction is disputed and untested. [C21, CD2] Practitioner, second-hand; inference.
- **Plan stall events carrying clues, not only hints, with a last one that closes the scenario anyway**, as a usually
  unsatisfying backstop, not a plan. A must-reason gate may hold longer: say how long, weighing what it teaches. [C20,
  C41] Practitioner, tabletop; inference.
- **Use red herrings knowing the dispute**: where players must act on a conclusion they latch on, so tabletop advice is
  "extreme caution"; the novel tradition allows them. Whether a game counts as the first case is inference. [C20, CD3]
  Practitioner, contested.
- **Treat "understood the mystery, stuck on a technicality" as a bug.** [C24, C29] Practitioner, single.
- **Track what the player has concluded, not only what they've seen**: tick a revelation only once they've accepted it.
  [C20] Practitioner; inference.
- **Consider checking answers in batches** to limit brute force; some players will still cycle guesses, and a menu
  leaks. [C23] Demonstrated, single on the exploit.
- **Test what happens when a player gets there first**: whether people enjoy being ahead is unmeasured. [M24] Measured,
  no evidence; inference.
- **Pick the playtest method knowingly** (interviews, numbers then tests, a retelling): no condition for choosing, nor
  hint practice, was found. [C24, C26, C48, CD15, C41] Practitioner, contested.

## First-person and unseen protagonists

- **Choose first person for voice, secrets and presence, not identification**: whose side the story takes matters, the
  pronoun not reliably. [M25] Measured: contested, leaning no reliable effect; moderate on whose perspective; prose,
  video and persuasion studies, none in a game. Presence was measured; voice and secrets are inference.
- **Don't justify silence by immersion**: silence and voice are both unmeasured, and a practitioner advises against
  silence for its difficulties. [M28, C43, CW6, CD9] Measured, no evidence; practitioner.
- **The more plot and relationship, the less silence carries**: it worked when people talked at a man of action and
  failed once a large cast drove events around him. [C44] Critic; inference.
- **With a fixed past and chosen voice, the player owns tone, disclosure and silence: write silence as a choice with its
  own lines**, since sporadic silence breaks the fiction when the other character chats on. [C45] Demonstrated, student
  account; the rule is inference.
- **Give a reason the player knows less or does little, not the amnesia an LLM likely defaults to**; a tourist
  witnessing others' stories is one. [C33, C46] Demonstrated; inference.
- **Let a protagonist's change be heard in delivery and small lines**, such as barks and animations changed at scripted
  points; no stated arc needed. [C47] Demonstrated, third-person.
- **Customisation has the only retention result nearby, and it conflicts with a fixed voiced lead.** It comes from one
  study of a breathing exercise, not a story; whether a cosmetic version keeps it is unknown. [M29] Measured: moderate
  for identification, weak for retention, no evidence in a story game with a fixed lead.

## Player-directed romance

- **Decide and state whether characters may simply not be into the player.** Practitioners split, both sides praised,
  and the only condition offered is what the story needs. [C63, CD10] Practitioner, contested; inference on the
  condition.
- **Keep content showing who a character is outside romance, so a player who declines loses no story.** [C64]
  Practitioner; inference.
- **Make romance a reaction to what the player chose, not a reward for enough chats** (the coin-slot version). [C64,
  C65] Practitioner and critic. Smith's remarks predate launch.
- **Give a non-romance relationship equal scenes.** [C65] Practitioner; inference.
- **Write "no thanks" as a line choice with an authored reaction**: don't punish it, don't let the character keep
  pressing, and stop romance-coded lines after a signalled no (audit item 6). No practitioner statement on it was found.
  [C66] Inference; the closest case is demonstrated, on a student account.
- **If the protagonist has an authored past, flirting is characterisation, not a reward**: it commits the player to
  something. [C67] Inference, one game.
- **Don't assume a romance keeps anyone playing**: bonds are correlational and sex-sells has no story measurement.
  Content limits, including the fixed exclusion, are in story-design. [M6, M35] Measured, no evidence for continuation.

## Voice and still images

None of this depends on player action; it sits here because the evidence comes from games and game writers load both
skills (D23).

- **Budget voice as a bet**: no experiment compares voiced with text-only dialogue on immersion, enjoyment or
  continuation. [M18] Measured, no evidence.
- **Time a short recorded scene with your real voices and budget from that**, not the vendor words-per-minute rule of
  thumb; nothing game-specific was found. [C54, CW10] Vendor, second-hand from search summaries; inference.
- **A voice is part of the character: a base voice set once, plus whatever they put on for a line.** Pitch and timbre
  follow the body unless a recorded reason (say comedy) says otherwise. An accent comes only from the character's
  history, holds in every line and never signals guilt, intelligence or job. A put-on voice (tough, sweet, imitating,
  mocking) is stated per line, an unasked one is a fault, and it must sound like the same person putting it on.
  Imitation keeps the imitator's voice with the other's mannerisms, and only of an invented character, never a real
  person. Owner's direction, one project; untested on any voice tool.
- **Test a generated voice against itself across feelings, not only against other voices**: distinct voices can still
  stop sounding like one person line to line. Give any speaker-similarity score a same-take control, and listen to one
  continuous clip of many lines as well as separate takes. The score should fall on put-on lines, doesn't measure
  feeling and disagreed with the owner's ear, so it orders takes to hear and never gates a voice. One project, a small
  sample, one listener who knew which test it was.
- **Test how long a still holds under a line**, since fixed order and time remove reader control. [C57] Inference; no
  writing on motion-comic pacing was found.

## Audit a draft

Derived rules, not measured: nobody tested this audit. Run it with `../story-design/CHECKS.md`, and keep the results
beside the draft.

1. **Conclusions.** Each is labelled must-reach (routes listed, none resting on a single success) or must-reason (no
   clue skips the steps). [C19, C24, CD1]
2. **Actions.** Each task or puzzle result has a row saying what it establishes and, in a mystery, who it implicates; a
   blank cell is a task beside the story. [C39]
3. **Promises.** Each thing the opening implies is delivered or deliberately subverted. [C40]
4. **Stalls.** Each must-reach sticking point has a named story event, the last closing the part anyway; each
   must-reason point states how long it may block and what follows. [C20, C24, CD1]
5. **First chapter.** The story-design first-chapter check, plus the first task or puzzle result fills an action row.
   [C39]
6. **Silence and decline.** Each flirtatious beat has a decline branch, each spoken choice allowing silence has lines
   for it, and later lines read both. [C45, C66]
